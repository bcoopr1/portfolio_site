Argus

2026 · Lead Engineer · LoRa · Python · Long Range Mesh Network

Foundry is a component library and design system built for a 120-person software company that had accumulated four years of inconsistent UI patterns across twelve product surfaces. Every team had their own button. Some had three. The design and engineering orgs were spending more time arguing about components than building features.

I led the architecture from scratch: a token-based design system where all visual decisions — spacing, color, radius, shadow — live in CSS custom properties defined once and consumed everywhere. The component library is written in React and TypeScript, with Storybook as the primary documentation surface. Every component ships with accessibility attributes, keyboard navigation, and a thorough story matrix covering all variants and states.

The hardest part wasn't the engineering — it was the adoption strategy. We used a "strangler fig" approach: the new components shadowed the old ones in the codebase, and teams migrated surface by surface over a four-month period. I wrote the migration guide and ran weekly office hours. Twelve months in, Foundry powers 100% of the company's product surfaces, and the design/engineering handoff process has been cut from three rounds of revision to one.
